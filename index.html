About Me

I am a researcher specialising in energy harvesting, vibration and structural dynamics, sensors, and self-powered systems. My work combines analytical modelling, numerical simulation, experimental testing, instrumentation, signal processing, and data analysis to investigate dynamic and electromechanical engineering systems.

My research spans piezoelectric and electromagnetic energy harvesting, linear and nonlinear dynamic systems, energy conversion, power optimisation, energy storage, and the integration of energy harvesting technologies with low-power sensing systems. I am particularly interested in developing practical technologies for autonomous sensing and engineering applications.

My broader research interests include energy harvesting, sensors, vibration, structural dynamics, energy storage and batteries, energy and functional materials, structural health monitoring, and intelligent engineering systems.

Background

My academic training spans mechanical engineering, railway engineering, structural dynamics, vibration, energy harvesting and sensing. I completed a BEng in Mechanical Engineering at the Federal University of Technology, Akure, followed by an MSc in Railway Engineering at Addis Ababa University. My doctoral research at the University of Southampton developed my expertise in vibration, structural dynamics, computational modelling, experimental testing and energy harvesting.

During my doctoral research, I undertook a research attachment at A*STAR's Institute of Materials Research and Engineering (IMRE), Singapore, where I worked on piezoelectric transducers, energy harvesting systems, experimental characterisation and sensing technologies. Alongside research, I have supported engineering education through laboratory teaching, tutorials, assessment and research communication.
